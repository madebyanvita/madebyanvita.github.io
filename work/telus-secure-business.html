Case study 07 · TELUS · B2B e-commerce

TELUS Secure Business: an online store for small-business security.

TELUS wanted small businesses, with up to 29 people, to be able to shop for a security system online. Through the agency Architech, I was the only designer on the project for four months. I scanned the market, mapped the customer's journey, and designed the flow from wireframes to final screens for desktop and mobile, then supported QA and the developers as it was built. Despite tight deadlines, it launched in December 2020.

Act I

The mess.

Security isn't an easy thing to buy online

The goal was an e-commerce flow for TELUS security services, aimed at the people who own or run small businesses. A security system isn't a simple product. There are plans to compare, hardware to choose, prices that depend on what's bundled, and the right service depends on where the business is. And at the end, a sales agent still needs to follow up.

The deadline was tight, so the team took an MVP approach for the first phase. My job was to work out what the store had to do first, and design it well.

The questionHow might we help a small-business owner choose the right security plan online, and hand them to a sales agent ready to buy?

Act II

The meaning.

Looking at the market first

Before designing anything, I made a visual market scan in Miro that everyone on the team could read. It covered TELUS's direct competitors, the largest security providers in Canada, and indirect ones that were more popular in the US. Then I looked for the features they had in common.

Mapping the journey

Next, I mapped how a small-business owner would get from a search to a call from a sales agent. It showed where the store sat inside TELUS's wider site, where the region decided which service applied, and where the sales team took over.

The work.

Three screens that do the work

Through to launch

I stayed with the project as it was built: visual QA and testing as each story was finished, and support for the developers along the way. We worked in an agile team, with developers, a QA engineer, the product owner and a content strategist at every stage of the design.

In spite of the tight deadline and a few roadblocks, we finished the project, and it launched in December 2020.

Learnings

What I'm taking with me.

  1. A visual scan gets everyone on the same page. Laying out what other companies did, side by side, made it easy for the whole team to talk about the same things.
  2. An MVP is about choosing. With a tight deadline, the hard part was deciding what the first version had to do, and what could wait.
  3. Stay close through the build. Visual QA as each story got built is part of the design work, not an afterthought.
